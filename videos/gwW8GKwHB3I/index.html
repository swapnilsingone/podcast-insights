By the numbers

Four numbers worth memorizing.

If you retain only four figures from this conversation, these are the ones that change how you read every AI-infrastructure story for the next five years.

Charts

Three pictures of the economics.

Compute demand moved two decimal places in two years. The factory math explains why expensive chips still produce cheap tokens. And the engineering bar is now measured in tokens, not salary.

Compute demand — three eras (log scale)

Each transition is 100× more compute. Most analyst models haven't caught up to this compounding.

Source: Jensen at 22:30–23:30 — "Generative to reasoning was 100×. Then agentic added another 100×. Ten thousand times in two years."

$50B factory cost breakdown

The GPU delta is smaller than assumed. Throughput — 10× higher — is the variable that changes the token economics.

Source: Jensen at 7:51–8:53 — land, power, shell ≈ $20B; storage, networking, cooling ≈ $15B; GPUs ≈ $15B.

Engineer economics — the token floor

Jensen's benchmark: a $500K IC spending less than $250K in tokens is leaving capability on the table.

Source: Jensen at 24:36–26:00 — "If that $500K engineer did not consume at least $250K worth of tokens, I am going to be deeply alarmed."
